## 0. Introduction

> This is the first post in a series about team 18, Wangganae Sikgudeul, at the 2026 AI Woowangthon. I wrote it so that someone seeing our project for the first time can get the "what, why and how" from this one post. The contest and the return are over, and part 17 closes the series. I last updated it on November 26.

When students build a web service in a project course, they [deploy](glossary:deployment) it themselves on a commercial [cloud](glossary:cloud-computing), and when they pass the free tier they pay out of their own pocket. When the course ends, the service usually goes down with it. Meanwhile the lab PCs sit idle outside class hours. So we decided to tie three school PCs together into a platform where students [deploy](glossary:deployment) for free.

In restaurant terms, the students are customers and the three school PCs are a borrowed kitchen. A customer hands in an order slip (the repo URL), an inspector checks the ingredients (the code) first, the owner decides whether to accept it, and the head chef hands the dish to one of two cooks. Customers only come and go through the front door, and the kitchen's back doors are all shut. The kitchen is the [cluster](glossary:cluster), the head chef is the [leader](glossary:control-plane), the cooks are the [workers](glossary:worker-node), the front door is [Cloudflare Tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel), the back doors are [ports](glossary:port), a boxed-up dish is a [container image](glossary:container-image), the inspector is AI, and the owner is the operator.

This is the first post of the series. It covers the contest theme and the problem we solve, the flow and structure of the platform, my role, where we are, and the series index. I took the [cluster](glossary:cluster) and finished all seven stages, we won the Excellence Award at the finals on November 21, and the return was done on November 26.

## 1. What is Woowangthon

It's a three-week [hackathon](glossary:hackathon) run by LIKELION Chungbuk National University and the Chungbuk National University AI Bootcamp program. There is one theme.

> **Can AI be a coworker?**

The organizers gave three examples. A coworker that keeps track of your graduation requirements, a coworker that checks in on elderly people, a coworker that catches defective products first. All three have the same shape: **AI filters and suggests first, but a person makes the final call**. They also ask "how is this different from what already exists?"

## 2. The problem we are solving

The users are students. When you build a web service in a project course, you [deploy](glossary:deployment) it yourself. You put it on a commercial [cloud](glossary:cloud-computing) like [Vercel](glossary:vercel), [AWS](glossary:aws) or [Cloudflare](glossary:cloudflare), and when you go over the free tier, you pay out of your own pocket. When the course ends, the service usually goes down with it.

| Problem                                              | Details                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Cloud](glossary:cloud-computing) costs are a burden | The moment you pass the free tier, money comes out of the student's own card                                                       |
| School computers sit unused                          | Lab PCs are off or idle outside class hours                                                                                        |
| Courses stop at [deployment](glossary:deployment)    | You learn to [deploy](glossary:deployment), but running it afterward (outages, [monitoring](glossary:monitoring)) is never covered |

## 3. What we are building

**A [deploy](glossary:deployment) platform where students run their own [GitHub](glossary:github) repos on a server made by tying school PCs together.** A student only submits a repo URL and pays nothing. Before a [deploy](glossary:deployment), AI checks the code for security risks first, and if a service starts acting strange while it runs, AI explains the cause in plain words.

From the student's side, one [deploy](glossary:deployment) goes like this.

1. The student logs in with a [GitHub](glossary:github) account, enters a public repo URL, picks a type (static web / server / web+server) and, if they use a DB, the connection details for their own DB, and submits
2. **AI reviews it.** It pulls the repo read-only and looks for risks like exposed secret keys or overly broad public permissions, with evidence. If something needs fixing, a bot account opens a [PR](glossary:pull-request) with a suggested fix
3. **The operator decides.** They read the review summary and pick one of approve, request changes, or block
4. Only approved code gets built into a [container image](glossary:container-image), on an isolated build server outside the school
5. The school PC [cluster](glossary:cluster) pulls that [image](glossary:container-image) and runs it. The app connects to the student's own DB with those details
6. Visitors come in through a `name.stairnoworks.com` address
7. If something goes wrong while it runs, like running out of memory or restarting over and over, rules stop or restart it first, and AI sends an alert explaining the cause and what to do next

## 4. What AI does, what people decide

This is our answer to the contest theme. We don't hand decisions over to AI.

| Situation                                | What AI does                                                                       | What people decide                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pre-[deploy](glossary:deployment) review | Finds security risks in code and config and reports them with evidence             | The student fixes or pushes back, the operator decides on approval |
| Metric anomaly in production             | Guesses the cause of the anomaly, explains it in plain words, suggests a next step | Picks the action: restart, stop, or [roll back](glossary:rollback) |

How we differ from what already exists also comes out of this table. On commercial [clouds](glossary:cloud-computing) the student pays, and commercial AI [deploy](glossary:deployment) helpers ([Vercel](glossary:vercel) Agent, [AWS](glossary:aws)'s security and ops agents, that kind of thing) are paid and aimed at companies. We run on school PCs so students pay nothing, and every [deploy](glossary:deployment) gets a review and anomaly analysis without any extra setup.

There is one more AI coworker in this series. It's the [AI coding agent](glossary:ai-coding-agent) ([Claude Code](glossary:claude-code)) that typed commands and edited the plan next to me while I set up the [cluster](glossary:cluster). It got some things right, and once it threw the design off course because of an assumption it made on its own. I write that down too and don't hide it.

## 5. Overall structure

The platform lives in the [cloud](glossary:cloud-computing) ([AWS](glossary:aws)), and only student services run on the school PCs.

| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Cloud](glossary:cloud-computing) ([AWS](glossary:aws)) | User Page, Admin Page                             | [React](glossary:react) + [TypeScript](glossary:typescript), [S3](glossary:amazon-s3) + [CloudFront](glossary:amazon-cloudfront) | [Deploy](glossary:deployment) screen for students, approval screen for operators                               |
|                                                         | Platform server                                   | [Nest.js](glossary:nestjs), [EC2](glossary:amazon-ec2)                                                                           | Takes requests, asks for builds, tells the school [cluster](glossary:cluster) to [deploy](glossary:deployment) |
|                                                         | AI server                                         | [FastAPI](glossary:fastapi), [EC2](glossary:amazon-ec2)                                                                          | Pre-[deploy](glossary:deployment) review, anomaly analysis                                                     |
|                                                         | Build                                             | [AWS](glossary:aws) [CodeBuild](glossary:aws-codebuild)                                                                          | Turns student code into [images](glossary:container-image). The only outside step where student code runs      |
|                                                         | DB · login                                        | [Supabase](glossary:supabase)                                                                                                    | [GitHub](glossary:github) login, stores review results and [deploy](glossary:deployment) status                |
|                                                         | [Message queue](glossary:message-queue)           | [Kafka](glossary:apache-kafka)                                                                                                   | Keeps [deploy](glossary:deployment) records from getting lost                                                  |

| School                                                  | [Cluster](glossary:cluster)                       | [k3s](glossary:k3s), 3 school PCs                                                                                                | Runs student services                                                                                          |
| School ↔ outside                                        | [Private network](glossary:private-network)       | [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale)                                                                                                  | How the platform server and operators reach the school PCs                                                     |
|                                                         | Public [tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel)       | [Cloudflare Tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel)                                                                                  | How visitors reach student services                                                                            |
| School                                                  | [Monitoring](glossary:monitoring)                 | [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus), [Grafana](glossary:grafana)                                                                   | [Node](glossary:node) and service metrics                                                                      |

There is one important assumption on the school PC side. **We don't open a single inbound [port](glossary:port) into the school.** The PCs sit behind the school [firewall](glossary:firewall), so nothing outside can start a connection to them. That means every connection to the outside is started by the school PCs, going out. Operators and the platform server come in over the [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) [private network](glossary:private-network), and visitors come in through the [Cloudflare tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel).

The [cluster](glossary:cluster) looks like this.

| PC   | Role                             | What it does                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| PC 1 | [Leader](glossary:control-plane) | [Cluster](glossary:cluster) control, public [tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel), [image](glossary:container-image) cache, metrics collection. Takes no student services |
| PC 2 | [Worker](glossary:worker-node)   | One student project                                                                                                                                                      |
| PC 3 | [Worker](glossary:worker-node)   | One student project                                                                                                                                                      |

The three are wired with LAN cables to one [switch](glossary:network-switch) we bought. If a [worker](glossary:worker-node) goes down, the [leader](glossary:control-plane) brings that project back up on the remaining [worker](glossary:worker-node). The DB is the student's own and lives outside the [cluster](glossary:cluster), so there is no data to move with it. There is only one [leader](glossary:control-plane), so if the [leader](glossary:control-plane) goes down, new [deploys](glossary:deployment) stop. With only three PCs, we accept that.
