## 0. Introduction

> Part 4 of the series. Build stage 2: remote access. Taking the [disk images](glossary:disk-image) is in [part 3](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-03-disk-images/), and the big picture is in [Woowangthon 0](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-00-overview/).

I had to manage three school PCs, kept in the club room, from home. I can't stay in front of the PCs for long, so the first job was a way to get in remotely.

In restaurant terms, the three PCs are three locked branch kitchens that I have to run from home. I need a passage past the front-door guard (the school [firewall](glossary:firewall)) to the kitchen, a key to use instead of a password, and a stove that lights itself again after a power cut. The passage is [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale), the key is a separate [SSH key](glossary:ssh-key) per device, and the stove is the [BIOS](glossary:bios) setting that powers the PC back on after power loss.

Before all that the internet had to work, and I almost concluded "the school blocks the internet" when it didn't. In this stage I did the wall [LAN](glossary:lan) [port](glossary:port) and [switch](glossary:network-switch) wiring, installed [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale), registered [SSH keys](glossary:ssh-key) on all three, set up [passwordless sudo](glossary:passwordless-sudo) for the [AI agent](glossary:ai-coding-agent), and ran an unattended reboot test.

## 1. On site, only what can't be done remotely

I can't stay in front of the PCs for long. So I split the work into three batches.

| Batch | Where                    | What                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A     | At each of the three PCs | Disk image (part 3), wired internet check, [SSH](glossary:ssh) server, [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale)                                                              |
| B     | Once, from the laptop    | Register [public keys](glossary:public-key) on all three, [passwordless sudo](glossary:passwordless-sudo). One [loop](glossary:network-loop) goes through all three |
| C     | Back at the three PCs    | [BIOS](glossary:bios) power setting, unattended reboot test                                                                                                         |

The rule was "only do at the PC what has to happen before remote access exists." From B on, the [AI coding agent](glossary:ai-coding-agent) on my laptop ([Claude Code](glossary:claude-code)) goes in over [SSH](glossary:ssh) and does the work. For that, A had to build the path and B had to make the key first.

## 2. Switch wiring and the wall port

The club room wall had two [ports](glossary:port). At first I plugged the [switch](glossary:network-switch) into one of them, but it turned out to be what looked like a phone [port](glossary:port). The [LAN](glossary:lan) [port](glossary:port) was the other one, and the club's Wi-Fi [router](glossary:router) was already using it.

One [port](glossary:port), two things that need it. So I connected the wall [port](glossary:port) to our [switch](glossary:network-switch) and moved the club's Wi-Fi [router](glossary:router) under the [switch](glossary:network-switch).

![Wiring labels on each switch port](/static/images/woowangthon-04-remote-access/switch-wiring.webp)

_From the top: wall [port](glossary:port), the club's Wi-Fi [router](glossary:router), worker 2, worker 1, leader. I labeled them so anyone can plug a cable back in if it gets pulled_

- The [router](glossary:router) connects to the [switch](glossary:network-switch) through its **[WAN port](glossary:wan)**. If you plug in a [LAN](glossary:lan) [port](glossary:port), the [router](glossary:router) may try to hand out its own addresses on the school network
- Only one cable goes to the wall. Two cables from the [switch](glossary:network-switch) to the wall make a [loop](glossary:network-loop), and that can take down the whole club room network
- The club's Wi-Fi [router](glossary:router) now hangs off our [switch](glossary:network-switch) too. If the [switch](glossary:network-switch) loses power, the club Wi-Fi goes down with it. The club members need to know this

## 3. I thought the internet was blocked

As I found in [part 3](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-03-disk-images/#7-what-i-checked-after-imaging), none of the three PCs has a Wi-Fi card. The only internet is the wall [port](glossary:port). The PCs also already had [static IPs](glossary:static-ip-address) assigned by the school. The question was whether the internet worked from this wall [port](glossary:port) with those [static IPs](glossary:static-ip-address) as they were.

It was a minimal install, so there wasn't even `ping`. I checked two things.

```bash
ip neigh
curl -sSI -m 5 https://1.1.1.1
```

`ip neigh` shows devices on the same network that have answered. The [gateway](glossary:gateway) answered, and its vendor was the school's network equipment. The cable was properly on the school network. But `curl` timed out. Port 80 did the same. [DNS](glossary:dns) answered once and then timed out on other names.

From these results alone it looked like "the school blocks outside internet for this [IP](glossary:ip-address)." I even made the next plan. Try getting an address automatically ([DHCP](glossary:dhcp)) behind the club's Wi-Fi [router](glossary:router), and if that fails, ask the school.

Before that, I changed just one address.

```bash
curl -4 -sSI -m 8 https://tailscale.com | head -1
```

```text
HTTP/2 200
```

The [Ubuntu](glossary:ubuntu) package server also returned 200. **The internet worked from the start.** What was blocked was `1.1.1.1` ([Cloudflare](glossary:cloudflare)'s public [DNS](glossary:dns) address), the target I picked for the test. I hit an [IP](glossary:ip-address) directly instead of a name to rule out [DNS](glossary:dns) problems, and that exact [IP](glossary:ip-address) happened to be blocked.

The lesson is simple. **If the test target is wrong, the diagnosis is wrong.** Now I check connectivity against the address of the service I'll actually use (`tailscale.com`). And when I write down results, I keep "no internet" and "can't reach 1.1.1.1" separate. The first is a conclusion, the second is an observation.

## 4. The path: Tailscale

The school PCs are behind a [firewall](glossary:firewall), so nothing outside can open a connection to them first. With [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com), you install a program on each device and log in with the same account, and it ties those devices together like one [private network](glossary:private-network). Each device opens a connection out to [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) first, so I didn't have to ask the school to open any [ports](glossary:port).

I installed it on each of the three and gave them names.

```bash
curl -fsSL https://tailscale.com/install.sh | sh
sudo tailscale up --hostname=k3s-leader
```

Open the login URL shown on screen on the laptop, approve it with the same account, and that's it. I named one machine wrong (`k3s-w1-1`) and fixed it remotely later.

```bash
sudo tailscale set --hostname=k3s-w2
```

### A relay, not a direct connection

After connecting, I checked the route and it wasn't direct. [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale)'s Tokyo [relay server](glossary:relay-server) ([DERP](glossary:derp)) was linking the school PCs and my devices, and round-trip [latency](glossary:latency) was 70 to 80 ms. It looks like the [firewall](glossary:firewall) or [address translation](glossary:nat) on the school network side keeps the two devices from making a direct connection, so traffic goes around through the [relay server](glossary:relay-server).

That's enough for remote access. 80 ms doesn't show when you're typing a few commands. This confirms why in [part 2](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-02-network-design/#2-tying-the-nodes-together-with-a-switch) I chose a [switch](glossary:network-switch) for node-to-node connections instead of [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) (option C). If every DB write had to go to Tokyo and back, it would be unusable. I'll measure the [latency](glossary:latency) again in stage 6, when the platform server sends deploy commands over this path.

[Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) device keys expire after 180 days by default. You can turn expiry off, but I left it. It's much longer than the contest, and I'll delete everything when I return the PCs anyway.

## 5. The key: a separate SSH key per device

I work from both a Windows desktop at home and a Mac laptop. I need to get into all three PCs from both.

The rule is **make a separate key on each device, and never copy a [private key](glossary:private-key).** If you move a [private key](glossary:private-key) from one device to another and lose one of them, you don't know which key to remove. With a key per device, you just delete one [public key](glossary:public-key) line for the lost device.

```bash
ssh-keygen -t ed25519 -f ~/.ssh/wanggane_k3s -N "" -C "<device name> wanggane-k3s"
```

I put the same connection block on both devices. `HostName %h` means use the name as is, since [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) resolves device names to addresses ([MagicDNS](glossary:magicdns)).

```text:~/.ssh/config
Host k3s-leader k3s-w1 k3s-w2
  HostName %h
  User <school PC account>
  IdentityFile ~/.ssh/wanggane_k3s
  IdentitiesOnly yes
  ServerAliveInterval 30
```

I added the two [public key](glossary:public-key) lines to all three PCs at once from the laptop. This is the only time each PC asks for a password.

```bash
for h in k3s-leader k3s-w1 k3s-w2; do
  ssh -o PubkeyAuthentication=no "$h" \
    'umask 077; mkdir -p ~/.ssh; cat >> ~/.ssh/authorized_keys' < ~/.ssh/wanggane_k3s_all.pub
done
```

## 6. Passwordless sudo for the AI agent

Here I needed one thing ready for my AI teammate. **I don't give the [AI agent](glossary:ai-coding-agent) passwords.** If you paste a password into a chat, it stays in the conversation history. But without a password, the install stops at every line with `sudo`.

So I put one file on each of the three PCs that allows [passwordless sudo](glossary:passwordless-sudo).

```bash
for h in k3s-leader k3s-w1 k3s-w2; do
  ssh -t "$h" 'echo "$USER ALL=(ALL) NOPASSWD:ALL" > /tmp/wanggane \
    && sudo visudo -cf /tmp/wanggane \
    && sudo install -m 440 -o root -g root /tmp/wanggane /etc/sudoers.d/wanggane; \
    rm -f /tmp/wanggane; sudo -k; sudo -n true && echo "NOPASSWD-OK"'
done
```

- Only a file that passes the syntax check with `visudo -cf` gets installed. A typo in a [sudo](glossary:sudo) config file breaks [sudo](glossary:sudo) itself, and remotely there's no way to undo it
- The setting lives in this one file only, so when I return the PCs I just delete this file
- I also fixed the removal order. If this file goes before [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale), removing [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) asks for a password and stops. The order for return day is written separately in the plan

After `NOPASSWD-OK` came back three times, I never had to type a password again. Turning off sleep, checking time sync and checking the specs of all three were done remotely by the agent on the laptop. That's also when I confirmed all three have identical specs.

```bash
sudo systemctl mask sleep.target suspend.target hibernate.target hybrid-sleep.target
```

This line that turns off sleep is the basics of running unattended. If a PC falls asleep in an empty club room, remote access falls asleep with it.

## 7. Power: coming back on after an outage

The last part was back at the PCs. If the power went out or someone unplugged and replugged the power strip, these PCs stayed off. The [BIOS](glossary:bios) "Restore after AC Power Loss" setting was `Power Off`. You can't change that remotely.

On all three, I took a photo of the screen before changing anything and switched it to `Power On`. The [BIOS](glossary:bios) isn't in the [disk image](glossary:disk-image), so I'll use these photos to set it back when I return the PCs.

Next was the **unattended reboot test**. I saved, rebooted, did nothing at the PC, and connected right away from the laptop.

```bash
ssh k3s-leader 'uptime'
```

Even without anyone logged in, [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) reconnected and [SSH](glossary:ssh) worked on all three. That means internet, [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) and [SSH](glossary:ssh) all come back without a human touching anything. I didn't actually pull the power cord as a test. I only changed the setting, so if a PC is ever off after an outage, this setting is the first thing to suspect.

That night I also confirmed from the Windows desktop at home that I could get into all three with the key and that `sudo -n` worked. That finished stage 2.
