## 0. Introduction

> Part 16 of the series. The [regression test](glossary:regression-test) on November 15, the day of the contest submission, when I ran the whole demo path again from start to finish. The leader's deploy executor registering with the [GoCD](glossary:gocd) server is in [part 15](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-15-gocd-registration/), and the big picture is in [Woowangthon 0](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-00-overview/).

On November 13~14 the team connected a deploy from the platform all the way to a student space for the first time. After that, 15 [PRs](glossary:pull-request) (#215–#232) were [merged](glossary:merge) into the team repo's `main`, and judging uses the `main` commit at 18:00 on submission day. Before recording the demo, I had to check once more that all ten demo steps still ran on that commit.

In restaurant terms, it was opening morning, and I ordered all ten dishes on the menu again from start to finish. With one customer at a time, all ten came out, but when two overlapped, the kitchen sent the second order slip back. The restaurant is the platform, the ten dishes are the ten demo steps, ordering them again is the [regression test](glossary:regression-test), the kitchen is the deploy [pipeline](glossary:deployment-pipeline) ([GoCD](glossary:gocd)), the line of order slips is the [queue](glossary:queue), and the slip sent back is the [409](glossary:http-status-code) response.

On the morning of November 15 I ran the demo path again from start to finish on the new `main`. 23 of 29 tests passed, 3 failed (B1 · B2 · B3), and 3 were observed · checked. B1 and B2 had the same shape: fine when running alone, broken only when overlapping with another deploy. I fixed those two that afternoon and they passed on a rerun. B3 doesn't affect the demo, so it stays as a known limit.

## 3. The AI fix that failed when overlapping

From here on, these are situations the demo doesn't have. In the demo, deploys run one at a time. In real use, several students press deploy at once. So I made two overlap.

In T33 I overlapped two deploys and let the AI fix them. One failed. The AI found the cause and made a fix commit, and that commit got through [build](glossary:build) and deploy to ready. But the test step that checks the fix (`run_verify`) fell over with `PIPELINE_UNAVAILABLE`, and the result ended as "user action needed." No fix [PR](glossary:pull-request) was opened either.

At the same time, in T34 I put two deploys in the [queue](glossary:queue) back to back. That passed. It waited at "1 ahead," then started when the [build](glossary:build) ahead finished. Next, in T35 I ran the AI fix [PR](glossary:pull-request) flow alone, and it succeeded.

Fine alone, broken in pairs. This is the cause I settled on. There is only one [pipeline](glossary:deployment-pipeline), `student-trial`, for trial deploys of the app the AI fixed. In [part 15](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-15-gocd-registration/#7-what-has-never-run) it wasn't even defined; it appeared in the meantime. If an earlier trial is using that [pipeline](glossary:deployment-pipeline), [GoCD](glossary:gocd) rejects the new run request with [409](glossary:http-status-code), and the platform server treats that as a failure right away.

But this is inferred. Whether a [409](glossary:http-status-code) really came back can only be known from the platform server's logs, and I had no access to the container logs on the [EC2](glossary:amazon-ec2). The [issue](glossary:issue) ([woowangthon/WOOWANGTHON_TEAM_18#239](https://github.com/woowangthon/WOOWANGTHON_TEAM_18/issues/239)) says "inferred" too. The evidence behind the inference is that T35 succeeded alone.

## 4. Operations alerts and leftover slots

Next were operations alerts. They work by the platform server pulling alerts from the leader's [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus) every 20 seconds. It uses [the service proxy path from part 13](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-13-metrics/#3-the-rights-say-yes-and-still-422) as is. So there was nothing new to change on the leader.

| Test | Input                                                 | Result                                                                                                                                       |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| T37  | `CHAOS=crash` (an app that dies as soon as it starts) | Repeated restarts, then an automatic stop. The student page shows 5 restarts, [exit code](glossary:exit-code) 1 and 20 log lines as evidence |
| T38  | `CHAOS=unready` (alive but never ready)               | An alert the AI judged from the state                                                                                                        |
| T39  | Stopping from an alert on the operator page           | Passed. One stop handled two alerts for the same project                                                                                     |

From the student page, all of it passed. Whether the alerts reached Discord I couldn't check, because I had no access to the channel. One small issue: the 20 log lines shown as evidence fill up with [health check](glossary:health-check) [503](glossary:http-status-code) lines, which pushes the actual cause out.

Then T40 caught one more. A service with no public address was raising 4 operations alerts. Following it back, it was the failed slot of an earlier first deploy that the AI had ended by opening a fix [PR](glossary:pull-request) or with "user action needed." That slot's [Pod](glossary:pod) wasn't taken down and kept restarting, raising alerts.

Looking at the code, the cleanup that takes failed slots down only ran when the AI gave up (`gave_up`). The design doc even gave a reason: with "user action needed," the [agent](glossary:ai-agent) has to read that slot's [Pod](glossary:pod) logs, so it's kept. But by then the ticket is already closed, and there's no [agent](glossary:ai-agent) left to read them. The slot stayed after the reason for keeping it was gone.

## 6. The one bug I left alone

While T41 was running, in T42 I sent three deploys to AI fixing at the same time. The third failed with `AGENT_UNAVAILABLE`.

This is less a bug than a limit we set. The AI server runs at most two at a time (`agent_max_runs`, default 2). It answers the third request with `503 AGENT_BUSY`, and the platform server treats that as a failure without retrying. Same shape as B1: reading "busy" as "failed."

I didn't fix this one. The demo runs deploys one at a time so it isn't affected, and I chose to spend the time left before submission on the B1 · B2 reruns. Instead of putting it in the repo, I decided to share it in the team channel as a known limit.
