## 0. Introduction

> Part 8 of the series. The first piece of build stage 5 (a frame for student apps): the day an [image](glossary:container-image) [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) went up on the leader. The leader GPU work is in [part 7](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-07-leader-vllm/), and the big picture is in [Woowangthon 0](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-00-overview/).

Student app [images](glossary:container-image) go to [GitHub](glossary:github)'s [container registry](glossary:container-registry), [GHCR](glossary:ghcr). So that the two workers don't each pull the same [image](glossary:container-image) over the school network, I put a [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) on the leader.

In restaurant terms, [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) is a faraway produce market, and the two workers are the kitchens of two branches. If each branch travels to the market on its own, it is slow, so the head office stocks a warehouse once and the branches pick up from there. The warehouse only goes to the market for what it lacks. If the warehouse door is open, a branch gets an immediate "we don't have it" and goes to the market; if the whole warehouse is dark, the branch waits at the door with no answer at all. The market is [GHCR](glossary:ghcr), the [container registry](glossary:container-registry), the branch kitchens are the worker nodes, the head office warehouse is the leader's [pull-through cache](glossary:pull-through-cache), the "we don't have it" is [connection refused](glossary:connection-refused), and no answer at all is a [timeout](glossary:timeout).

I put a [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) on the leader that pulls from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) on the workers' behalf, and the second worker got the same [image](glossary:container-image) in 1.1 seconds. I also tested that nodes pull straight from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) when the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) is off, and an AI reviewer pointed out that a [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) being off and a [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) not answering are different. Measured again, that cost 30 more seconds.

## 1. Why a cache

When a student deploys, the build runs on [AWS](glossary:aws) and the [image](glossary:container-image) goes to [GHCR](glossary:ghcr). A worker pulls that [image](glossary:container-image) and runs it. With two workers, the same [image](glossary:container-image) gets pulled twice at times, for example when one worker goes down and the app moves to the other.

[GHCR](glossary:ghcr) was slow from the school network, in the same way that [one worker's download stalled](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-06-k3s-install/#6-one-workers-download-stalled) in part 6. So the leader pulls from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) once and the workers take it from the leader over the switch (about 940 Mbit/s).

The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) is a [pull-through cache](glossary:pull-through-cache). When a node asks for an [image](glossary:container-image), the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) hands over what it has; if it has nothing, it fetches from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr), passes it on, and keeps it for 7 days. The product is the open-source [registry](glossary:container-registry) [Docker](glossary:docker) started ([CNCF Distribution](glossary:cncf-distribution), `registry:3.1.2`). One config file is enough, and student [images](glossary:container-image) are public, so no [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) login is needed.

## 2. Where to bind the cache

The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) listens on the leader's node address, `192.168.77.11:5000`. [Kubernetes](glossary:kubernetes) has two ways to open a [Pod](glossary:pod) on a node's port.

| Way         | How                                                                        | [Firewall](glossary:firewall)                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| hostPort    | Traffic to the node port is rewritten to the [Pod](glossary:pod)'s address | The rewritten path [goes around](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-06-k3s-install/#3-closing-servicelb-and-nodeport) the [firewall](glossary:firewall)'s inbound check |
| hostNetwork | The [Pod](glossary:pod) uses the node's network as is (same address too)   | The [firewall](glossary:firewall) applies exactly as it does to a program running on the node                                                                                   |

I picked hostNetwork, and had the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) listen on the node address only. Nothing on the school address is accepted in the first place, and the [firewall](glossary:firewall) lets in only the node network.

There is one price. The [Pod Security Standards](glossary:pod-security-standards) level planned for student spaces ([baseline](glossary:pod-security-standards)) forbids hostNetwork, so only the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache)'s space has that level lifted. In return the container runs as a normal user, with no special privileges ([capabilities](glossary:linux-capability)) and a [read-only](glossary:read-only-filesystem) file system. The level is lifted for one reason only: using the node's network.

## 3. Making nodes look at the cache first

On all three nodes I put `/etc/rancher/k3s/registries.yaml`. It is four lines.

```yaml
mirrors:
  ghcr.io:
    endpoint:
      - 'http://192.168.77.11:5000'
```

[k3s](glossary:k3s) reads it and writes the settings the container runtime ([containerd](glossary:containerd)) uses. In the result the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) is a `host` and [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) is the `server`. The runtime tries the `host` first and the `server` last.

![hosts.toml for ghcr.io written by k3s. The cache address is a host, and the original https://ghcr.io is the server. containerd tries hosts first and the server last](/static/images/woowangthon-08-image-cache/hosts-toml.svg)

That `server` line is what sends pulls to [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) when the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) is dead. Nothing else to set.

Changing this file means restarting [k3s](glossary:k3s). I did it one node at a time, w2 → w1 → leader, [in the order from part 5](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-05-node-prep/#3-put-the-undo-in-place-before-it-breaks). Restarting [k3s](glossary:k3s) leaves running containers alone, so zero [Pods](glossary:pod) restarted. On the leader, the [Kubernetes](glossary:kubernetes) API was back in 2 seconds.

## 4. One second for the second worker

The test [image](glossary:container-image) was a small public [image](glossary:container-image) on [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) (34.8 MB). I started it on w1 first, and on w2 as soon as that finished.

![Image pull records of the two test Pods. w1 took 1 m 53 s, w2 took 1.1 s](/static/images/woowangthon-08-image-cache/pull-events.svg)

The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) log confirmed it. The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) writes a line each time it fetches something new from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr): nine lines for w1, none at all for w2. w2 got everything from the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache).

With the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) scaled to zero and the [image](glossary:container-image) removed from w1, the next pull had the runtime's connection to the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) [refused](glossary:connection-refused), moved straight on to [GHCR](glossary:ghcr), and finished in 44 seconds. From the school address, port 5000 on the leader could not be reached. All three done-criteria passed.

## 5. The first pull was slower through the cache

Putting the numbers side by side shows something odd.

| Path                                                                           | Time     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------- |
| Through the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache), first (empty)                | 1 m 53 s |
| From the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) (second worker)                  | 1.1 s    |
| Straight from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) ([cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) off) | 44 s     |

The very first pull through the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) was more than twice as slow as a direct pull. The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) adds a step while it fetches from [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) and passes things on, and [GHCR](glossary:ghcr)'s speed from the school network also varied (section 6). It is one measurement, so I did not pin down the cause. The fix is clear anyway: pull the [images](glossary:container-image) the demo needs once beforehand, and these 1 m 53 s never show up in front of the judges. It is in the docs as a known limit.

## 6. What the reviewer AI caught: refused vs no answer

I opened the [PR](glossary:pull-request), and the review bot ([CodeRabbit](glossary:coderabbit)) didn't come. Instead it left a note: "this repository does not receive automatic reviews because it has fewer than 10 stars." Until last week it reviewed every [PR](glossary:pull-request) on its own, so the bot's free plan seems to have changed. Repos under 10 stars now need a comment to call it. After a comment calling it in, three findings came. All three were right.

| Finding                                                                                                                                         | Right?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The doc says the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) can only go out on 443 and 53, which differs from the [firewall](glossary:firewall) table | Yes. Programs on the node also have 80, time sync and [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) ports open. 443 and 53 are just what the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) actually uses                                                                                   |
| The 50Gi [volume](glossary:volume) is not a limit                                                                                               | Yes. The storage in use ([local-path](glossary:local-path-provisioner)) is a plain folder on the node and doesn't cap size. The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) has no size limit either, so 7 days is the only cleanup. Watch what piles up with a disk alert |
| "Pulls go straight to [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) when the leader is down" was never tested                                                           | Yes. Below                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

The third one stung the most. What I tested in section 4 was the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) scaled to zero. The leader was still on then, so it answered [connection refused](glossary:connection-refused) right away because nothing was on port 5000. A [connection refused](glossary:connection-refused) makes the runtime move to the next address at once. But when the leader loses power, nobody is there to refuse. The runtime opens a connection to an address that never answers, and waits. [Refused](glossary:connection-refused) and no answer are different.

So I faked a dead leader. On w1 I made packets to the leader's port 5000 get dropped silently ([DROP](glossary:packet-drop)) and pulled the same [image](glossary:container-image). Just in case, I first armed a timer that removes the rule by itself after 5 minutes.

![containerd log on w1. With the cache off: connection refused, moved on at once, 44 s. With no answer: i/o timeout after 30 s, 216.6 s in total](/static/images/woowangthon-08-image-cache/fallback.svg)

It moved to [GHCR](glossary:ghcr) after waiting 30 seconds, and the whole pull took 3 m 37 s. Even minus those 30 seconds that is 3 m 6 s, while the same [image](glossary:container-image) took 44 seconds a few minutes earlier. [GHCR](glossary:ghcr)'s speed from the school network changes from one moment to the next. The docs now say "when the [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) can't be reached, every pull first waits 30 seconds; this was not tested by actually powering off the leader."

The docs in this [PR](glossary:pull-request) were written by an AI agent, and the wrong sentences were caught by an AI reviewer. The contest theme is "Can AI be a colleague?", and this time the colleagues reviewed each other. Calling the reviewer in and pressing the merge button were still done by a person.

## 7. A question left in the deploy flow

The same night, the deploy flow design by the AI teammate (suda5936) was merged. Its steps are named W0 to W7.

- **W4 build** goes as far as building the [image](glossary:container-image) on [AWS](glossary:aws) and pushing it to [GHCR](glossary:ghcr). The [cache](glossary:pull-through-cache) from this post pulls that [image](glossary:container-image).
- **W5 deploy**: the leader's runner takes the [image](glossary:container-image) tag and the deploy settings, renders the [Kubernetes](glossary:kubernetes) files and applies them. Same as [the meeting decision in part 7](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-07-leader-vllm/#1-what-changed-in-the-meeting).
- **W6 verify** has not settled. The design doc's last commit moved it to the platform server ([AWS](glossary:aws)), but right after the merge the AI teammate said on Discord that keeping it on the leader with W5 would be better. The team lead (TaeTaeJin) asked for a settled answer.

From the cluster side, the leader is easier. The runner account designed in part 7 already includes read access for verification, and no new path from [AWS](glossary:aws) to student apps would be needed. One question is left: is it a single check at the end of a deploy, or does an ongoing watch after the deploy move to the leader too? The latter adds an always-on program to the leader. The plan marks it as "not decided yet."

The first piece of stage 5 is done. Next is the student space template: making the space student apps run in, with resource caps and blocking rules. The cap values have to be agreed with the platform server owner first.

## 8. Reflection

[Refused](glossary:connection-refused) and no answer are different, so fake a failure the way it will really happen.
