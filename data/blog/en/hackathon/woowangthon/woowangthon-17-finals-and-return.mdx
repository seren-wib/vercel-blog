## 0. Introduction

> Part 17 and the last post of the series. The regression test on submission day is in [part 16](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-16-regression-test/), the [disk images](glossary:disk-image) taken at the start for this very return are in [part 3](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-03-disk-images/), and the big picture is in [Woowangthon 0](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-00-overview/).

The contest ended on November 21, and the three borrowed school PCs had to go back in the state we received them. Deleting, one by one, what I had installed over three weeks would be easy to get wrong, so I chose to overwrite them with the [disk images](glossary:disk-image) taken at the start.

In restaurant terms, it is emptying a borrowed kitchen before handing it back. You restore it to the photos taken before opening, take out the recipe notes you want to keep, cancel the credit accounts with suppliers, and return the keys last. The kitchen photos are the [disk images](glossary:disk-image) taken with [Clonezilla](glossary:clonezilla) plus the [BIOS](glossary:bios) photos, the recipe notes are the leader's [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus) metrics data, the credit accounts are the [Cloudflare Tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel)s, keys and certificates left in the cloud, and the keys are the device entries in the [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) admin console.

We submitted with the 18:00 commit on November 15, and at the finals on November 21 team 18 won the Excellence Award (School of Electrical Engineering Head's Award). That evening I moved the leader's metrics to an external disk, deleted what was left in the cloud, and overwrote the three PCs. Removing them from the admin console on November 26 finished the return on the school side.

## 3. What set the return order

![Return order. On November 21: back up the leader data, clean up the cloud, Clonezilla restoredisk on three PCs, put the BIOS back. On November 26: remove the three PCs from the admin console](/static/images/woowangthon-17-finals-and-return/return-order.svg)

This is where the reason for choosing a [disk image](glossary:disk-image) over "deleting things one by one" in part 3 pays off. I don't have to delete, item by item, the [k3s](glossary:k3s), student data, public keys, sudo settings and [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) installed on the three PCs over three weeks. One restoredisk brings a disk back to the state imaged on November 4, which is the state we received.

There are things an image can't erase, though. They set the order.

| Order | What                               | Why it has to come first                                                                                                 |
| ----- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1     | Back up leader data                | restoredisk erases the leader disk. Anything worth keeping has to leave first                                            |
| 2     | Cloud cleanup                      | [Cloudflare Tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel)s, keys and certificates live outside the disk. They survive an overwrite |
| 3     | restoredisk ×3                     | Back to the disk as received                                                                                             |
| 4     | Put the [BIOS](glossary:bios) back | [BIOS](glossary:bios) settings live on the motherboard, not on the disk                                                  |
| 5     | Remove from admin console          | Delete only after seeing that the three will never come back                                                             |

## 4. What I kept before the overwrite

The leader held about 10 days of cluster metrics that [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus) had collected since the night of November 11. An overwrite would erase them, so they came out first.

| File                  | Size    | Content                                                                                  |
| --------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `prometheus-tsdb.tar` | 4.76 GB | [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus) data. 17 blocks, November 11 22:54 ~ November 21 18:39 |
| `grafana-data.tar`    | 58.6 MB | [Grafana](glossary:grafana) dashboards and settings                                      |
| `cluster-state.txt`   |         | Cluster state right before the overwrite                                                 |
| `prometheus-cr.yaml`  |         | [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus) configuration                                          |

I stopped [Prometheus](glossary:prometheus) and [Grafana](glossary:grafana) before copying. Archiving a data folder while it is being written can capture a half-written block. The copy took 16 seconds, and I remounted the external disk and compared [SHA-256](glossary:sha-256) values. All three files matched. This backup currently lives on that one external disk only.

## 5. Cloud cleanup

These are the things left outside the disks. I handed this job to a [Claude Code](glossary:claude-code) session, and I checked the list of what to delete and decided how to proceed.

| Where                                                   | What was deleted                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Cloudflare](glossary:cloudflare)                       | 4 [DNS records](glossary:dns-record) ([wildcard](glossary:wildcard-dns-record), `api.`, `app.`, certificate validation), 2 [tunnels](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel) (the leader's visitor entrance, the [EC2](glossary:amazon-ec2)'s `api.` entrance) |
| [AWS SSM](glossary:aws-systems-manager-parameter-store) | 8 parameters. [Tunnel](glossary:cloudflare-tunnel) token, [vLLM](glossary:vllm) API key, [GoCD](glossary:gocd) auto-register key, admin password and notify token, the server account's connection details                                         |
| [GoCD](glossary:gocd)                                   | Scaled the leader executor to [replicas](glossary:replica) 0, then disabled and deleted the leader [GoCD Agent](glossary:gocd) on the server                                                                                                       |
| AWS [ACM](glossary:acm)                                 | Detached the `app.` address and certificate from [CloudFront](glossary:cloudfront), and deleted the certificate once nothing used it (18:47)                                                                                                       |
| [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale)                         | 1 [EC2](glossary:amazon-ec2) device, 6 shares on the three nodes (one teammate plus one share link per node)                                                                                                                                       |
| AWS [Elastic IP](glossary:elastic-ip)                   | Confirmed none was ever created                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

The [ACM](glossary:acm) certificate didn't delete right away. Deletion is refused while a [CloudFront](glossary:cloudfront) distribution still uses the certificate. So I detached it from [CloudFront](glossary:cloudfront) first, and deleted it after that change had deployed and the certificate's users (`InUseBy`) dropped to 0.

The student service images ([GHCR](glossary:ghcr)) and the [registry](glossary:container-registry) write token belong to the bot account, so that account's owner cleans them up. The [Grafana](glossary:grafana) view-only password went away with the leader disk.

## 6. Overwriting the three PCs

On site, I booted each PC from the [Clonezilla](glossary:clonezilla) USB and ran `restoredisk`. The source is the image taken in part 3, and the target is the internal [NVMe](glossary:nvme). From imaging on November 4 to the overwrite on November 21 is 17 days.

| PC       | Image                        | Target           | Check                                                  |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Worker 1 | `k3s-w1-orig-2026-11-04`     | `nvme0n1` 931.5G | Image check 3 min 44 s (9.00 GB/min), Cloning finished |
| Worker 2 | `k3s-w2-orig-2026-11-04`     | `nvme0n1` 931.5G | Target confirmed with `lsblk` before the restore       |
| Leader   | `k3s-leader-orig-2026-11-04` | `nvme0n1` 931.5G | Target confirmed with `lsblk` before the restore       |

The same trap as imaging sits here, pointing the other way. In [part 3](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-03-disk-images/#5-taking-the-images) the device names differed from PC to PC. Picking the wrong target this time would overwrite the external disk that holds the images. So before each restore I checked size and model with `lsblk` and picked only the 931.5G internal [NVMe](glossary:nvme) as the target.

For worker 1 I recorded the check time and the completion screen. For worker 2 and the leader I only confirmed that the restore and boot finished, and didn't keep a record of the target disk screen or the check time.

## 7. BIOS reset and the admin console

I had changed one thing in the [BIOS](glossary:bios). In [part 4](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-04-remote-access/#7-power-coming-back-on-after-an-outage) I set "Restore after AC Power Loss" to `Power On` so the PCs would come back after an outage. On all three I set it back to `Power Off` as in the part 3 photos, saved, booted normally, and saw the same screen as when we received them.

With [Tailscale](glossary:tailscale) wiped by the overwrite, the three PCs sat offline in the admin console from November 21. On November 26 I removed them under Machines in the console (6 devices to 3). The `k3s-*` entries in the [ACL](glossary:acl) made for the nodes and the teammate share rule went out the same day, when I rewrote the [ACL](glossary:acl). The return stage's done criterion, "all three exactly as in the first images, [BIOS](glossary:bios) matching the photos", closed here.

One [disk image](glossary:disk-image) taken at the start stands in for most of the return list, so prepare the return on day one.
