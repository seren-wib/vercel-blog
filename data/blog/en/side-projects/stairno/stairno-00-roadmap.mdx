## 0. Introduction

Run separately, my freelance work, games, and small programs and web services would mean three names, three sites and three accounts. For one person that is the same work done several times, so I tied them under one name. This post covers the overall structure and order, and the first job, the shared design package.

In restaurant terms, Stairno is the name of a restaurant chain and the four branches are the four sites. Instead of a membership card per branch there is one card that works in all of them, and the head-office counter that issues cards and takes payments is the account center and the shared [API](glossary:api). The signboard and interior manual that every branch follows the same way is the [Design system](glossary:design-system), and a branch's address is its [Domain](glossary:domain). The name comes from _stairno_, "star" in Gothic; read in English it contains _stair_, so I chose it for a star climbing one step at a time from the ground. What I did: fixed the site structure and the account policy, wrote a 10-step roadmap, and pulled the look of the hub `stairno.com` out into the `@stairno/ui` package.

## 1. Four sites, one account

![Stairno's overall structure](/static/images/stairno-00-roadmap/structure.svg)

_Solid lines are live today, dashed lines are planned (the labels in the diagram are in Korean)_

| Site                 | Role                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **stairno.com**      | **The hub.** Introduces Stairno and sends people to each site. Sells nothing              |
| **stairnoworks.com** | **Works.** The freelance desk: requests, quotes, deposits, progress                       |
| **stairnogames.com** | **Games.** The home of the game Vane                                                      |
| **stairnolabs.com**  | **Labs.** Catalog and store for products. Desktop programs are bought and downloaded here |

They do different things, so they are separate sites. All four [domains](glossary:domain) were bought on 17 October 2026.

Four sites easily turn into four sign-ins and four payment systems. A sign-up form, a [Payment gateway](glossary:payment-gateway) and a refund process per site means doing the same work four times, with four times the bugs and security holes. So I decided to build it like an Amazon account.

- **One Stairno account** signs you into every site and product except the hub
- **Register a card once**
- See purchases, subscriptions and downloads in the **account center** (`account.stairno.com`)
- Payments, subscriptions and entitlements are handled in **one shared [API](glossary:api)** (`api.stairno.com`)

The other sites just call these two, which is why every arrow in the diagram gathers in the middle band. The hub has no account screens and no payments.

| 4    | Account center, part 2: payments ([Test mode](glossary:test-mode)) | Card registration, one-time payments, subscriptions, refunds, payment mail. No real money moves                         | ✅ Done |
| 5    | stairnolabs.com                                                    | Product catalog → payments and downloads. The first site to connect to Stairno accounts                                 |         |
| 6    | stairnoworks.com                                                   | Service introduction → requests, quotes, deposits                                                                       |         |
| 7    | stairnogames.com                                                   | Vane introduction and dev diary                                                                                         |         |
| 8    | First product launch                                               | Open atlas-odyssey, a learning roadmap service, for free with Stairno accounts                                          |         |
| 9    | Business registration → real payments                              | Sign a [Payment gateway](glossary:payment-gateway) contract and swap the test keys for real keys                        |         |
| 10   | Vane                                                               | Starting from one playable section                                                                                      |         |

Why this order:

- **The design package comes first.** So that every site begins with the same look as the hub. Building sites first and matching the look later means fixing every site
- **The account center and [API](glossary:api) come before the sites.** Every other site calls these two. They are the only servers, and the sites start as introduction pages and add sign-in and payments later only where needed
- **Real payments are step 9.** Taking real money requires business registration and a [Payment gateway](glossary:payment-gateway) contract first. Until then everything runs in [Test mode](glossary:test-mode), and the design makes switching a matter of swapping keys
- **Works comes after Labs.** A freelance site becomes useful once reviews have piled up. Until then, freelance work comes through freelance platforms

## 3. Working principles and tools

Four principles run through the whole series.

1. **Can one person run it?** One language ([TypeScript](glossary:typescript)), servers someone else manages, fixed costs close to zero
2. **Documents first.** Before code, write the project plan, [SRS](glossary:srs) and [SDD](glossary:sdd). If documents and code disagree, fix one of them in the same change
3. **Tests first.** With [TDD](glossary:tdd), write a failing [Software testing](glossary:software-testing) case first, then make it pass
4. **Check in production.** At the end of each stage, go through the [Acceptance criteria](glossary:acceptance-criteria) item by item on the real service address

The tools: [Astro](glossary:astro) (the hub and account center screens), [Hono](glossary:hono) and [Better Auth](glossary:better-auth) (the shared API), [Cloudflare Workers](glossary:cloudflare-workers) and [Cloudflare Pages](glossary:cloudflare-pages) (every site and the API), a [Database](glossary:database) on [Neon](glossary:neon)'s [PostgreSQL](glossary:postgresql) used through [Drizzle](glossary:drizzle), mail through [Resend](glossary:resend), payments through [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) (won) and [PayPal](glossary:paypal) (dollars), tests with [Vitest](glossary:vitest), and automation with [GitHub Actions](glossary:github-actions). Most of the code was written by an [AI coding agent](glossary:ai-coding-agent), [Claude Code](glossary:claude-code). I set the scope and made the decisions, checked the results, and did what a person has to do, such as dashboard settings and entering cards.

## 4. Step 1: the shared design package

### The starting point and the problem

Before step 1 there was the hub, a [Static site](glossary:static-site) built and published in a single day on 17 October: pages prebuilt with [Astro](glossary:astro) and uploaded to [Cloudflare Pages](glossary:cloudflare-pages), with no sign-in and no [Database](glossary:database). The design is black and white only, a night sky and a staircase drawn in dots with a star climbing the stairs. The font is [Pretendard](glossary:pretendard). It has Korean/English switching, light/[Dark mode](glossary:dark-mode) switching (following the computer's setting at first) and [Responsive design](glossary:responsive-design).

On 18 October I decided "every Stairno site looks exactly like the hub", but the hub's look was mixed into the hub's code. If each new site copies it, changing one header button means making the same change on the other three, and forgetting one leaves every site slightly different a few months later. So the look is managed in one place only: `@stairno/ui` (repo `stairno-ui`).

### What was pulled out

| Pulled out                            | Content                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Design token](glossary:design-token) | Named values for colors, spacing and corner rounding, used like `--bg` (background color) |
| Base styles                           | Fonts, text sizes, content width, fixed header, footer, button shapes                     |
| Header and footer                     | The site passes in the text and links                                                     |
| Theme switch button                   | Switches between light and dark and remembers the choice                                  |
| [i18n](glossary:i18n)                 | Links that move between `/ko` and `/en`                                                   |
| [Font loading](glossary:font-loading) | Where and how to fetch [Pretendard](glossary:pretendard)                                  |

The dot lettering and night-sky animation are the hub's own first impression, so they stayed in the hub; floating dot letters would only get in the way in the account center. The shared parts do not know "what to show". The header only draws the menu items it is given, and the site decides what goes in, so the hub and the account center can have different menus while sharing one header.

### Installing with a pinned version

Other sites install the [Package](glossary:package) straight from the [GitHub](glossary:github) repo with [npm](glossary:npm) and always pin a [Git tag](glossary:git-tag).

```bash
npm install github:seren-wib/stairno-ui#v0.2.2
```

Without a tag you get the latest code at that moment, so the hub runs yesterday's code, the account center runs today's, and the look changes although nobody bumped a [Semantic versioning](glossary:semantic-versioning) number. With a tag each site records "I use v0.2.2". Changing the look goes in this order: change `stairno-ui` and test it, put a new version tag on it, then bump the [Dependency](glossary:dependency) on each site.

### Did a single pixel change?

The success condition was "the hub looks as if nothing changed". It was checked in three layers.

1. **Comparing output files.** Built the hub before and after: the HTML differed only in a script's indentation, and the CSS only in two blocks of the same rule merged into one
2. **[Visual regression testing](glossary:visual-regression-testing).** 12 shots, light and dark × desktop (1280px) and mobile (360px) × the Korean, English and [HTTP status code](glossary:http-status-code) 404 pages, compared with a [Pixel diff](glossary:pixel-diff): **all 12 had zero pixels of difference**
3. **Behavior and [Unit test](glossary:unit-test).** Clicked the theme button, checked that the choice is remembered, the header changing on scroll and the language links by hand. Tests passed: 118 in the hub and 43 in the package

In the early hours of 18 October `stairno-ui` `v0.1.0` was released and the change that switched the hub over to it was merged. Step 1 done.

### Adding the account menu

That afternoon, while working on step 3, I added an account menu to the package. The original decision was no sign-in related screens on the hub, but once the account center existed it felt awkward that the brand's front page did not show whether I was signed in or where the account center was. So the hub gets one round button on the right of its header; account screens and payments still stay off the hub. It has to look exactly the same on every site, so it went into the shared package.

- **Signed in**: a button with the first letter of the name. Clicking opens a card with the name, the email, "Manage account" and "Log out"
- **Signed out**: a person icon. Clicking shows "Log in" and "Sign up"
- **Sites without a server, like the hub**: the browser asks the shared [API](glossary:api) directly who is signed in. If there is no answer within 3 seconds, or it fails, it shows the signed-out state
- **[Keyboard navigation](glossary:keyboard-navigation)**: opening it moves [Focus](glossary:focus) to the first link, and Esc or a click outside closes it and returns Focus to the button ([Accessibility](glossary:accessibility))

I built a fresh practice site and took 16 shots across signed in / signed out / hub mode × light and dark × desktop and mobile.

| Version  | What changed                                                                                           |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `v0.1.0` | The first version pulled out of the hub                                                                |
| `v0.2.0` | Account menu added                                                                                     |
| `v0.2.1` | Fixed an empty band under the footer on short pages                                                    |
| `v0.2.2` | Sites can choose where the brand logo points. In the account center, clicking the logo goes to the hub |

What is left is [Font loading](glossary:font-loading). While the [Pretendard](glossary:pretendard) stylesheet loads, drawing stops for about 0.9 seconds. It is the main reason the account center's sign-in page scored 91 in English and 81–85 in Korean for [Lighthouse](glossary:lighthouse) performance, short of the target of 90. Drawing first without waiting for the font is an open [Issue](glossary:issue) in `stairno-ui`.
