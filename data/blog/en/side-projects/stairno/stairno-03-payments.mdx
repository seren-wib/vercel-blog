## 0. Introduction

This is roadmap step 4. I added payments to the shared API, then checked that they really work in production before closing the step. The big picture is in [Stairno 0. The whole roadmap](/en/blog/side-projects/stairno/stairno-00-roadmap/). In payments a mistake is a money incident, so rules came before code. And since passing tests guarantee neither production addresses nor notices, I ran a separate rehearsal that plays scenarios in production.

In restaurant terms, the site is the dining room, the API is the kitchen, and the cashier is the payment company. A guest places an order and pays, the kitchen talks only to the cashier, and once the bill is settled it hands out an entry ticket. Register a regular's card once and it is charged every month on its own, and before opening day the staff play guests and run a whole day in advance. The cashier is the [payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway) (here [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) and [PayPal](glossary:paypal)), the entry ticket is the [entitlement](glossary:entitlement), the regular's card is the [billing key](glossary:billing-key), the monthly charge is [subscription](glossary:subscription) renewal, the interpreter at the cashier is the [adapter](glossary:adapter-pattern), and the dress rehearsal is the rehearsal.

From the afternoon to the night of 18 October I built payments in [sandbox](glossary:sandbox) and shipped `v0.4.0` and `v0.4.1`. In the early hours of 20 October I ran 10 scenarios in production. Right at the start it turned out that sign-in from other sites had been broken since launch day. 8 passed on the first night and the last two the next day, and step 4 closed on 22 October with `v0.4.3`. No real money moved.

## 1. What gets built, and by what rules

| [Payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway) | Currency         | What was built                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments)      | Korean won (KRW) | One-time payments, card registration ([billing key](glossary:billing-key)), automatic [subscription](glossary:subscription) charges, cancellation, [webhooks](glossary:webhook) |
| [PayPal](glossary:paypal)                    | US dollars (USD) | One-time payments, [refunds](glossary:refund), [webhooks](glossary:webhook)                                                                                                     |

The account center gets card registration, deletion and choosing the default card, order history, cancelling and resuming [subscriptions](glossary:subscription), and downloading purchased programs again. Receipts, failed renewal notices and [refund](glossary:refund) results go out by mail. Switching to real payments is designed to take nothing but swapping the test keys for real keys and adding the business details. That switch is step 9, after business registration. All the rules are in the repo's working rules, and the AI agent follows them too.

1. **Providers live only behind an [adapter](glossary:adapter-pattern).** The order code knows only common operations like "approve" and "cancel", not whether it is [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) or [PayPal](glossary:paypal). Tests run on a [fake adapter](glossary:test-double) instead of a real provider and can create situations like "decline the next payment" or "pretend the provider is down" at will
2. **Amounts are an integer plus a currency.** 1,000 won is `1000 KRW` and 1 dollar is `100 USD` (cents). No decimal arithmetic, and won and dollars are never added together
3. **There is no code that takes card numbers.** The [payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway)'s screen takes them. We keep only the encrypted [billing key](glossary:billing-key), the card issuer and the last four digits
4. **The server re-checks the amount.** If the amount sent by the browser differs from the order amount, it is refused and no approval request goes out
5. **[Webhooks](glossary:webhook) are [idempotent](glossary:idempotency).** The same notice arriving twice is processed once
6. **Orders follow a [state machine](glossary:state-machine).** Only allowed moves happen
7. **A [kill switch](glossary:kill-switch) turns off payments only.** In an outage, sign-in stays up and only payments stop
8. **No personal data in logs.** Order numbers and provider numbers instead of emails, [tokens](glossary:token) or card data

Here is one purchase of a 1,000 won program. (Labs does not exist yet, so the rehearsal site in section 6 stands in for it.)

1. The site sends its site [token](glossary:token) and the buyer's [token](glossary:token) together and creates an order. The API reads the price from the DB and makes the order `pending`. The site does not set prices, and an unpaid order expires after 30 minutes
2. The user enters a card and authenticates in the [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) window. No money has moved yet
3. The site sends the API "finalize this". The API checks in order whether it is this site's order, whether the payment number was already handled (if so it returns the first result as is), whether the order is still `pending`, and **whether the amount equals the order's**. If not, the order is set to `failed` and no approval request goes to [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments)
4. The API asks [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) to approve. The money moves here, and the approved amount is compared once more
5. Payment done, order `fulfilled` and [entitlement](glossary:entitlement) created are recorded in one go, and a receipt goes out in the user's language
6. When the [webhook](glossary:webhook) arrives later the payment is already handled, so nothing changes. If the user closed the window after step 2, this [webhook](glossary:webhook) does steps 3-5

## 3. What I ran into while building

### No interactive transactions

Normally the payment record, the order status and the [entitlement](glossary:entitlement) go into one [transaction](glossary:database-transaction). But the HTTP connection from Cloudflare [Workers](glossary:cloudflare-workers) to [Neon](glossary:neon) treats each request separately, so a [transaction](glossary:database-transaction) spanning several requests cannot be opened. So every state change is written as one [SQL](glossary:sql) statement with the condition "change it only if the order is in one of these states". The allowed-state list is generated from the [state machine](glossary:state-machine) table. Even if two requests try to change the same order at once, only the one that gets there first succeeds.

### Webhooks: verify before saving

Saving "received" first lets a forged notice take a real notice's event number, and when the real one arrives it is discarded as "already received". So the order is verify, save, process. [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) payment notices carry no signature, so on receipt we ask [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) directly about that payment and trust only the status it answers with.

### Signing download links ourselves

Files live in [R2](glossary:cloudflare-r2). Making a 10-minute download address in [R2](glossary:cloudflare-r2) needs one more key, and I did not want more [secrets](glossary:secret). So the address is the API's own, and the API signs "this file, until this time". When the user arrives it checks signature and time and streams the file.

### Telling a decline from an outage

"Card limit exceeded" is a decline: close the order as `failed` and tell the user. "The provider's server is not answering" is an outage: leave the order and allow a retry. I checked the error codes in the docs one by one so the [adapter](glossary:adapter-pattern) tells the two apart by code.

## 4. Why I moved to Git Flow midway

Up to step 3, a PR that passed [CI](glossary:ci) went straight to `main`, and `main` went straight to production. With payments half in, going to production is not the same weight as a changed letter on the sign-in screen. On the evening of 18 October, with over 20 payment PRs in, I moved to [Git Flow](glossary:git-flow).

- Everyday work collects in `develop`, and going into `develop` does not deploy
- When one roadmap step ends, a `release` branch is cut and sent to `main`, and that is the single deploy
- An urgent production bug is fixed with a [hotfix](glossary:hotfix) that touches only the production version
- The middle number of the [version number](glossary:semantic-versioning) is the roadmap step. The first release of step 4 is `v0.4.0`

Checks come in layers: only the changed tests per commit, all of [CI](glossary:ci) when pushing to `develop`, [CI](glossary:ci) required to reach `main`, tests once more right before deploy, then the rehearsal in production. That last layer is section 6 onward, because the address the [payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway) returns to and the [webhook](glossary:webhook) can only be truly checked in production.

## 5. v0.4.0 and v0.4.1

Around 10 pm, with payment mail, order history and the re-download screen in, I shipped `v0.4.0`. Then I saw that the account center was scattered across profile, devices, connected sites, payment methods, [subscriptions](glossary:subscription), orders, downloads and deletion. I compared the account screens of Google, Microsoft, Apple, GitHub and Kakao and changed it.

- Four blocks on one page: account, payment, my products, security. Clicking the left contents scrolls there and marks the block in view
- Account deletion at the very bottom, hard to press by mistake
- Password change, which was missing. Changing it signs out every other device and sends a "your password was changed" mail
- Country/region and time zone (order dates shown in it), profile photo

Birthday, gender, phone number and address are not collected. Not collecting personal data you have no feature for is [data minimization](glossary:data-minimization), a principle of the Korean privacy law, and it also leaves less to leak. Just after midnight I shipped `v0.4.1`, and that is the build. What remained was checking it works in production.

## 6. A rehearsal-only site

Tests run in a fake [payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway), a fake DB and a fake server. But whether the user who finished paying returns to the real site address, whether [webhooks](glossary:webhook) reach the real server and whether mail reaches a real mailbox can only be confirmed in production. So step 4 closes only after the scenarios are run one by one and the [acceptance checklist](glossary:acceptance-criteria) is filled in.

The catch was that creating an order needs a site connected to the API. An order request needs both a site [token](glossary:token) and the buyer's [token](glossary:token), the return address must be that site's https address, and the payment window opens on the site's page. The first site, Labs, is step 5. In step 3 I deferred a similar case with "check it once a site exists", but not this time. If something broke while attaching payments to Labs, I could not tell whether it was a bug in the payment API or in Labs.

So I built a one-page [Worker](glossary:cloudflare-workers) that only rehearses, at `experiments/payment-rehearsal` in the repo.

- It signs in exactly like a real Stairno site, with [OIDC](glossary:oidc) (including [PKCE](glossary:pkce)), and receives a site [token](glossary:token)
- Buttons: sign in, one-time product order (won via [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments), dollars via [PayPal](glossary:paypal)), confirm payment, confirm payment with the amount halved, start a [subscription](glossary:subscription), look up orders and [entitlements](glossary:entitlement). There is no card number field
- The site password (`client_secret`) lives only as this [Worker](glossary:cloudflare-workers)'s [secret](glossary:secret)
- **The API has no rehearsal exceptions or back doors at all.** The rehearsal only means something if it takes the same road as a real site. The tool stays in the repo and is rerun on every release that changes the payment path

Setup was: deploy the tool to Cloudflare, register it in the account center as an [OIDC](glossary:oidc) site and put the issued name and password into its [secrets](glossary:secret), register two products (1,000 won and 1 dollar one-time) and a monthly [subscription](glossary:subscription) (1,000 won), check the [webhook](glossary:webhook) addresses in the [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) and [PayPal](glossary:paypal) dashboards, and create a rehearsal account. I also wrote a script that reads the production DB. Roles: I enter cards and passwords and change dashboard settings, and the AI agent presses buttons and checks results (responses, DB records, mail).

Two things snagged. The site password issued at the first registration was printed in the working conversation log as is, so I deleted that registration and registered again, and confirmed that signing in with the deleted password is refused (`invalid_client`). Also, a DB account made in the [Neon](glossary:neon) console joins the admin group automatically, so "read permission only" had no effect. Instead the script opens its [transaction](glossary:database-transaction) as a [read-only transaction](glossary:read-only-transaction), which blocks writes at the source. It prints no emails, [tokens](glossary:token) or card keys.

## 7. A 404 on the first button

I pressed sign in in the rehearsal tool and the account center's `/sign-in` was a [404](glossary:http-status-code). That page is where other sites land when they press "Sign in with Stairno". Since launch day on 18 October no site had been able to sign in with Stairno, and nobody knew because no site was attached. It was exactly the check I had deferred to step 5 in step 3.

The cause: the account center prefixes addresses with a language ([i18n](glossary:i18n)), and [Astro](glossary:astro)'s setting was "prefix every page with a language", which renders a page without one and then turns it into a [404](glossary:http-status-code). The [OIDC](glossary:oidc) sign-in address is the language-less `/sign-in` that other sites send without knowing the user's language. Screen tests render a page in isolation, so address handling from the language setting never enters. "`/sign-in` renders the form" passed, while "this address is alive in the production build" was checked by no one.

In [Astro](glossary:astro), the only thing that can answer outside the language prefix is an address injected separately by an extension (integration). So I added `/sign-in` that way and sent the request to the sign-in page in the user's language (`/ko/sign-in` etc.) with a [302](glossary:http-status-code). Addresses that must answer without a language are tested as a list, and I started a real build to check that `/sign-in` gives a [302](glossary:http-status-code) and other addresses still give a [404](glossary:http-status-code).

## 8. Mail pointing at a deleted address, and v0.4.2

Preparing "press the button in the mail sent when a renewal fails", I saw that the button pointed at `/billing`. That is an address I deleted myself when `v0.4.1` in section 5 made the account center one page. While changing the screen layout I had not looked at the links inside mail. I changed it to the payment block of the new structure (`/ko/#payment-methods`).

I bundled the two fixes onto the production `v0.4.1` as [hotfix](glossary:hotfix) `v0.4.2`, with none of the other changes piled up in `develop`. [CI](glossary:ci), usually a few minutes, took close to 40 minutes that day because [GitHub Actions](glossary:github-actions) was slow, so push to merge took an hour. `v0.4.2` reached production around 1:30 am, and after checking `/sign-in` I restarted the rehearsal.

## 9. A secret nobody had kept

The original value of the production server's `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` was nowhere. Not in the [password manager](glossary:password-manager), not on my computer, and Cloudflare [Workers](glossary:cloudflare-workers) does not show a [secret](glossary:secret) again once it is set. The [signing key](glossary:jwks) that signs sign-in [tokens](glossary:token) (JWKS) is stored encrypted in the DB with this value, so nothing I had could decrypt it.

Before launch the only users were me and a test account, so I replaced the value.

1. Save the new value in the [password manager](glossary:password-manager) first
2. Put it into Cloudflare with [wrangler](glossary:wrangler) (`wrangler secret put`)
3. Delete the [signing key](glossary:jwks) encrypted with the old value from the DB
4. Fetch the [public key](glossary:jwks) address once so a new [signing key](glossary:jwks) is created, and check that it decrypts with the new value

Changing this value signs out every login and invalidates every download link already out. With users I would have had to announce it first. A [secret](glossary:secret) written down nowhere is a lost [secret](glossary:secret), and I moved this procedure into the operations doc.

### Moving refund requests into the account center

Scenario 3 exposed a gap in the design. The procedure said "request a [refund](glossary:refund) in the account center", but the account center had no [refund](glossary:refund) button. The [requirements spec](glossary:srs) had [refund](glossary:refund) requests come from the site and the account center only show status, so the code matched the spec and the procedure was wrong. I made the rehearsal pass by adding a "request [refund](glossary:refund)" button to the tool.

But from the user's side, whichever site they bought from, requesting in the account center that holds the order history is natural, and building a [refund](glossary:refund) screen on every site is the "same work many times" that [Stairno 0](/en/blog/side-projects/stairno/stairno-00-roadmap/#1-four-sites-one-account) set out to avoid. So I changed the decision on the spot and wrote it into the plan.

- [Refund](glossary:refund) requests are taken on the order detail in the account center. Sending a reason makes a request, and it does not [refund](glossary:refund) immediately
- The operator approves or rejects, and the result goes out by mail. The path for a site to submit a request stays as an entrance
- The [refund](glossary:refund) policy text lives on the selling site, and the account center links to it
- The spec is fixed first, then built, in the next release

## 11. The last two (21 October)

**Scenario 10.** I switched the account language to English and redid 1 and 3 with an easy-pay method. The order became `fulfilled` and an English receipt arrived, and requesting a [refund](glossary:refund) and approving it by operator command gave `refunded`, [entitlement](glossary:entitlement) revoked and an English [refund](glossary:refund) mail. Mail language follows the account language at the moment of sending, so the first night's Korean result could not prove the English side.

**Scenario 6.** The [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) test environment approves every payment whatever the card, so failures had to be made. The official way, a test marker on the request, needs an API change and breaks the "no rehearsal exceptions" rule. So I changed only the rehearsal data in the production DB so the stored [billing key](glossary:billing-key) and customer number no longer matched, and [Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) genuinely refused with "customer information does not match" (`NOT_MATCHES_CUSTOMER_KEY`). I changed the daily renewal [cron](glossary:cron) to every minute for a while, and since a retry normally waits three days after the last attempt, I set "last attempt date" back three days for each retry. Our side took this as a payment decline, not an outage, and handled it with a failed order and an English mail, and the mail button went to the account center payment section fixed in section 8.

| Attempt       | [Subscription](glossary:subscription) state | Failure count |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| First renewal | `past_due`                                  | 1             |
| Retry 1       | `past_due`                                  | 2             |
| Retry 2       | `past_due`                                  | 3             |
| Retry 3       | `expired`                                   | 4             |

After `expired`, the [subscription](glossary:subscription) [entitlement](glossary:entitlement) still looked set to its original expiry (a month later). The rehearsal had moved only the period end and left the [entitlement](glossary:entitlement) expiry alone, and in reality by the time a renewal fails that time has already passed.

I added a per-minute [Cron Triggers](glossary:cron-triggers) entry in the dashboard and waited 15 minutes with nothing happening, and only after losing 30 minutes did I open the logs. When [cron](glossary:cron) runs, the API writes one `renewal run` line even with nothing to renew, and since adding the per-minute [cron](glossary:cron) there was not a single one. That morning at 9:00 (midnight UTC) there was, so the original [cron](glossary:cron) was fine. The cause: I pressed "Add" in the add window and never pressed "Save". The first run came about 8 minutes after Save. The procedure now says "Save after Add, reload and check that it stayed".

## 12. Closing step 4 (22 October)

With the rehearsal all passed, cleanup and the release were still left.

- **Cleanup.** I deleted the check script that read the production DB, the connection file, and the allow rule that ran it without asking, and removed the temporary DB account in the [Neon](glossary:neon) console. The rehearsal tool stays
- **Shared docs.** The source is the repo's markdown, and Google Docs are aligned once when a step ends. I did not upload anew to keep the links and fixed only the changed places in the same documents. The [design spec](glossary:sdd) was three lines, the plan a few table rows and three decisions, the test and deployment doc a whole rehearsal section and all the [acceptance checklist](glossary:acceptance-criteria) results, and the [requirements spec](glossary:srs) was already correct. With a three-day step that was fine, and a longer one needs one more alignment midway
- **[Refund](glossary:refund) procedure.** Look at open [refund](glossary:refund) requests in the DB, judge by the site's [refund](glossary:refund) policy and approve or reject by operator command. Checking the code, refunding by hand in the [payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway)'s dashboard makes the [webhook](glossary:webhook) set the order to refunded but does not close the open [refund](glossary:refund) request. So the procedure says "do not [refund](glossary:refund) by hand in the dashboard"
- **[Webhook](glossary:webhook) replay.** A [webhook](glossary:webhook) that failed while processing stays in the DB as failed and the answer is an error, so the [payment provider](glossary:payment-gateway) resends it ([Toss Payments](glossary:toss-payments) up to seven times over 3 days 19 hours). After fixing the cause and deploying, resend from the dashboard and you are done, and scenario 8 already showed it is handled without duplicates
- **v0.4.3.** It shipped what had piled up in `develop`: removing the underline on order-list links, the [refund](glossary:refund) request button in the rehearsal tool, and one [flaky test](glossary:flaky-test). The test that checks a forged [billing key](glossary:billing-key) is refused changes the second-to-last character of the [ciphertext](glossary:ciphertext) to `A` (to `B` if it is already `A`), but it decided "is it already `A`" by looking at the neighboring character. When the character to change is already `A`, nothing changes, decryption succeeds and the test fails. That is about 1 in 64. I fixed it to look at the very character it changes
- **Two issues left open.** I wrote "Closes #209, closes #211" in the `v0.4.2` PR body, yet two [GitHub issues](glossary:github-issues) stayed open. GitHub closes issues with that keyword only when the PR merges into the [default branch](glossary:default-branch). This repo's [default branch](glossary:default-branch) is `develop` and release PRs go to `main`, so the keyword does nothing. I confirmed the fixes in production and closed them by hand

What remains is the [refund](glossary:refund) request in the account center and keep-me-signed-in (both spec first), plus three bugs found in the rehearsal: the hub account menu's "sign in" link points at an address that does not exist, "connected sites" in the account center is still empty after an [OIDC](glossary:oidc) sign-in, and a refunded order shows the [refund](glossary:refund) mark twice in the list. Then comes step 5, stairnolabs.com, where the production experiment step 13 deferred from step 3 and the "[token](glossary:token) refresh fails after account deletion" check also get done.
