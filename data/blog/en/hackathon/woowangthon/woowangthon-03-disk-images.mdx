## 0. Introduction

> Part 3 of the series. Build step 1: [disk image](glossary:disk-image) backups. The design story is in [part 2](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-02-network-design/), and the big picture is in [Woowangthon 0](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-00-overview/).

The three PCs we borrowed from school have to go back after the hackathon. I can't remember everything I will change over 3 weeks, so I decided to save the as-received state in one piece before installing anything.

In restaurant terms, handing back a borrowed kitchen means leaving it exactly as it was, so you photograph the whole kitchen before opening. The borrowed kitchen is the school PCs, the kitchen photo is the [disk image](glossary:disk-image), the camera is [Clonezilla](glossary:clonezilla), and the safe that keeps the photos is the external [NVMe](glossary:nvme) disk.

In this post, before even logging in, I booted each PC from USB and imaged its internal disk to an external disk. Along the way the [BIOS](glossary:bios) screen did not show up, device names were different on every PC, and one PC already had a changed password, so I took its [image](glossary:disk-image) again.

## 1. Why an image first

There are two ways to put a PC back the way it was when you return it.

| Method                                | How                                                                          | Catch                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Undo things one by one                | Remove installed packages, created accounts and changed settings from a list | Whatever is missing from the list stays. I can't remember everything I changed over 3 weeks |
| **[Disk image](glossary:disk-image)** | Image the whole disk at the start, write it back when returning the PC       | 20-30 minutes per PC at the start, and an external disk to store it on                      |

I kept the one-by-one approach as a fallback only and went with [images](glossary:disk-image). There is one condition. **The [image](glossary:disk-image) has to be taken before anything changes.** One account or one package and it is no longer the "as-received state". So the plan was to boot straight from USB without even logging in.

Some things don't go into the [image](glossary:disk-image). [BIOS](glossary:bios) settings live on the motherboard, not on the disk. So I decided to photograph the [BIOS](glossary:bios) screens before changing anything.

I don't know yet whether restoring the PCs is actually a condition for returning them. I left that as a question for the person who lent us the PCs, and prepared as if it were required.

[Ventoy](glossary:ventoy) is a tool that lets you keep several [ISO](glossary:iso-image) files on one USB and pick one at boot. You don't have to burn the USB again for every [ISO](glossary:iso-image).

[Clonezilla](https://clonezilla.org) is a free tool that clones a whole disk or saves it as an [image](glossary:disk-image). It skips free space and only compresses and saves the used parts, so even a 1TB disk ends up as an [image](glossary:disk-image) of a few dozen GB.

## 3. The BIOS screen doesn't show up

I got stuck on the very first PC. To boot from USB I needed the [boot menu](glossary:boot-menu) or the [BIOS](glossary:bios), but when I turned the PC on there was no vendor logo and no [BIOS](glossary:bios), just the login screen. There were two causes.

- **Where the monitor was plugged in.** The monitor was on the motherboard's [HDMI](glossary:hdmi) port. This PC has a separate [graphics card](glossary:graphics-card) (RTX 3080), so the motherboard output didn't show the early boot screens. When I moved it to the [graphics card](glossary:graphics-card)'s [HDMI](glossary:hdmi), the [BIOS](glossary:bios) screen appeared. On all three PCs the monitor has to go into the [graphics card](glossary:graphics-card)
- **[Fast Boot](glossary:fast-boot).** [Fast Boot](glossary:fast-boot) was on in the [BIOS](glossary:bios), so the boot went past before it took any keyboard input. Neither the key you press at the logo nor the [boot menu](glossary:boot-menu) key worked

For the first PC I ended up logging in once and entering the [BIOS](glossary:bios) with `sudo systemctl reboot --firmware-setup`. That command sends the next boot to the [BIOS](glossary:bios) setup screen. So this one PC got one login record before its [image](glossary:disk-image) was taken. The login record is the only thing that changed. From the next PC on, I plugged the monitor into the [graphics card](glossary:graphics-card) from the start and pressed the [boot menu](glossary:boot-menu) key at the logo (F11 on this motherboard) to pick the USB directly.

## 4. The button not to press

There was one trap I had written down while preparing. If you boot [Ventoy](glossary:ventoy) on a PC with [Secure Boot](glossary:secure-boot) on, a blue "Verification failed" screen comes up and asks you to enroll a key. If you enroll it, the boot works, but **the key stays in the PC [firmware](glossary:firmware).** Restoring the [disk image](glossary:disk-image) doesn't remove it. That would leave a mark on someone else's PC that I can't erase.

So the plan was: if that screen comes up, don't enroll, photograph the [BIOS](glossary:bios) screen, and turn off only [Secure Boot](glossary:secure-boot) for a while. In practice [Secure Boot](glossary:secure-boot) was already off on all three PCs, so I never saw that screen.

## 5. Taking the images

This is the order I picked in [Clonezilla](glossary:clonezilla).

1. `device-image`: save a disk as an [image](glossary:disk-image) file
2. `local_dev`: the destination is the plugged-in external disk. The folder is the top level (`/`)
3. `Beginner` → `savedisk`: save the whole disk
4. [Image](glossary:disk-image) name: `<PC name>-orig-2026-11-04`
5. Source disk: the internal [NVMe](glossary:nvme) `nvme0n1`
6. Default compression, **check the saved [image](glossary:disk-image): yes**, no encryption

![Clonezilla saving the internal disk](/static/images/woowangthon-03-disk-images/clonezilla-saving.webp)

_partclone reading the [root partition](glossary:root-partition). Only 48.6GB of the 956GB [partition](glossary:partition) is in use, so it finishes quickly_

A mistake at step 5 is game over. If you swap the source and the destination, the external disk overwrites the internal one. And **the device names were different on each PC.** On one PC the [Ventoy](glossary:ventoy) USB was `sda` and the external disk was `sdb`, but on another the external disk showed up as `sda`. So I checked by size, not by name. 115GB is the USB, 477GB is the external disk, 931GB is the internal disk. Only the internal disk starts with `nvme`, so there was at least less room for confusion there.

I always turned on the check at step 6. When saving is done, [Clonezilla](glossary:clonezilla) reads the [image](glossary:disk-image) back and confirms it can be restored. An [image](glossary:disk-image) that fails the check can't be trusted, so it has to be taken again. Finding out on return day that the restore doesn't work would be too late.

| PC       | [Image](glossary:disk-image) size | Notes                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Leader   | 19G                               | Saved the [EFI](glossary:efi-system-partition), boot and [root partitions](glossary:root-partition). Skipped the [swap partition](glossary:swap-space) (normal) |
| Worker 1 | 9.6G                              | Passed the check                                                                                                                                                |
| Worker 2 | 11G                               | Taken again the next day (section 6)                                                                                                                            |

All three together came to under 40GB. The 512GB external disk is now the only place that holds the originals of all three PCs, so I don't use it for anything else.

## 6. A password that was already changed

Worker 2 had a problem. After taking the [image](glossary:disk-image) I tried to log in, and the password from the person who lent us the PCs didn't work. It turned out a teammate had logged into this PC earlier and changed the password.

That means the [image](glossary:disk-image) I had just taken was not the "as-received state". If I wrote it back on return day, the person who lent us the PCs couldn't log in with their own password. So I set this order.

1. Get the changed password from the teammate and log in
2. Ask the teammate whether anything else changed besides the password (installed packages, changed settings)
3. Set it back to the original password with `sudo passwd`
4. Log out and confirm that the original password works again
5. Take the [image](glossary:disk-image) again under the same name

![Worker 2 image passing the check](/static/images/woowangthon-03-disk-images/clonezilla-checked.webp)

_The retaken worker 2 [image](glossary:disk-image). restorable on each [partition](glossary:partition) means it can be restored_

The [image](glossary:disk-image) I took again the next day passed the check, and the old [image](glossary:disk-image) was overwritten. I overwrote it under the same name because there should be only one [image](glossary:disk-image) holding the "as-received state", so nobody gets confused.

## 7. What I checked after imaging

Only after the [images](glossary:disk-image) were taken did I log in and look inside the PCs.

| Check                                                     | Result                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| OS                                                        | [Ubuntu](glossary:ubuntu) 24.04.5 [LTS](glossary:lts) on all three. Minimized server install with no desktop                                                                             |
| Where [root](glossary:root-partition) lives (`findmnt /`) | An [ext4](glossary:ext4) [partition](glossary:partition) on the internal [NVMe](glossary:nvme). An installed OS, not a live USB                                                          |
| Already installed                                         | [SSH](glossary:ssh) server, [Docker](glossary:docker), [open-iscsi](glossary:iscsi). [open-iscsi](glossary:iscsi) is needed later for volume replication ([Longhorn](glossary:longhorn)) |
| Wired LAN                                                 | All three have a free 2.5G [LAN port](glossary:ethernet). No USB LAN adapter needed                                                                                                      |
| **Wi-Fi**                                                 | **None of the three has a wireless card**                                                                                                                                                |

The last row mattered. One of the fallback plans from part 2 died here. If the wall [LAN port](glossary:ethernet) had no internet, each PC was going to go out through the school Wi-Fi, but with no Wi-Fi card that path doesn't exist. Now internet depends on a single wall port. That story is [part 4](/en/blog/hackathon/woowangthon/woowangthon-04-remote-access/).

## 8. Reflection
